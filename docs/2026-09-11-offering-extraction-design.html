Coach Assistant · Design spec · 11 Sep 2026

Offering extraction

Turn "workflows living in the founder's head" into real cp_offerings rows, in one box, in about sixty seconds, without the coach ever filling in a form. Precondition for P1 Enrollment Lifecycle.

North star gate. The coach never operates software. A wizard with five cards of fields fails this gate even though it produces perfect data. Every decision below is made against it.

1 · What already exists (do not rebuild)

The object model is further along than the P1 plan implies. Verified in lib/types.ts today:

FieldStatus
OfferingKindExists. Eight values, already shaped for live containers: one_on_one, private_intensive, mens_group, womens_group, mixed_group, private_retreat, online_retreat, custom
capacity, price_cents, starts_at, ends_atExist on Offering
OfferingMemberStatusExists: active | paused | completed | dropped. Most of an enrollment lifecycle
Waitlist, interested, alumniMissing. P1 territory, out of scope here
Promise / waiverMissing entirely. See §7, this is a hard dependency

Usage check: OfferingKind is referenced in exactly two files, lib/types.ts and components/clients/AddOfferingForm.tsx. The vocabulary is built and almost nothing uses it. This spec is largely about feeding it.

2 · The decisions

QuestionDecision
What are the answers for?Create real objects. The interview writes cp_offerings rows, it is not a profile
How do we collect them?One box. Speak or type, AI parses. Same pattern as /leads/capture
How much do we ask?Infer policy from shape, confirm exceptions. Do not ask what the shape already tells us
Where does it live?In the welcome flow, between magic and leads
How does confirm work?One plain-language summary, one confirm, with editable cards as the escape hatch

Placement reasoning: "what do you run" is the easiest question a coach can answer, it is their identity. "Who could you be talking to" is emotionally harder, which is why that step needed a reframe. Easy before hard. Offerings also give the leads step somewhere to point.

3 · The flow

Step A, the ask

What do you actually run? Say it however it comes out. A circle, a retreat, 1:1 work, all of it. I'll sort the details.

One textarea plus the existing voice input. No fields, no add-another button.

Step B, the parse

Model call returns a draft array of offerings. Each draft carries name, kind, capacity, price_cents, cadence, and a confidence flag per field. Nothing is written yet.

Step C, the summary and the assumptions

So: a Tuesday men's circle, 8 men, $200 a month. A retreat twice a year. And 1:1 work. Here is what I'll assume unless you tell me otherwise. The retreat takes a deposit and needs a waiver signed before anyone travels. The circle keeps a waitlist once it is full. The 1:1 work needs neither. Sound right? · Let me fix that

Highlighted text is gated. The waiver clause ships only once the Promise object exists. Until then the summary omits it. See §7.

Showing the assumptions back is the point, not a courtesy. It is how the coach learns what the machine will do on their behalf, and it is what makes inference safe rather than presumptuous. Assumptions buried in settings are a different product.

Step D, the escape hatch

"Let me fix that" reveals one editable card per offering: name, shape, capacity, price, dates, and the policy toggles. Add and remove rows. This is approach A from the options, available to everyone and paid for by nobody who does not need it.

5 · Data flow

  • Parse is a pure mapping from model JSON to draft rows, in lib/offering-extract.ts. No I/O, fully testable.
  • Inference is a second pure function, inferPolicy(kind), returning the row above.
  • Only on confirm does the component POST to a new /api/coach/import-offerings, mirroring /api/coach/import-leads.
  • Policies are stored on the offering. Until the Promise object exists (§7) the waiver flag is recorded, not acted on.
  • Skip writes nothing and is always one click.

6 · Error handling

Both failure shapes below are likely, not hypothetical.

InputBehaviour
One word: "coaching"Not enough to guess a shape. Fall back to three chips: Mostly 1:1 · A group or circle · Retreats. One tap creates one offering with defaults and a placeholder name the coach can rename later
A paragraph of philosophy with no offerings in itParse returns empty. Say so plainly and show the same three chips. Never invent an offering from vibes
Model call fails or times outSame three chips, plus the raw text preserved in the box so nothing the coach typed is lost
Parse returns a shape we do not haveMap to custom and keep their words as the name
Ten offerings claimedShow the first five, collapse the rest behind "and 5 more". Do not block

7 · The dependency that must not be fudged

Step C tells the coach "the retreat needs a waiver signed before anyone travels." The Promise object does not exist. cp_waiver and cp_agreement have zero references in the repo.

If we ship that sentence before Promise exists, the product has made a commitment it cannot keep, on the screen whose entire purpose is earning trust.

Ruling: until Promise ships, the summary says only what the app can do today. Waiver inference is stored on the offering and stays silent in the copy. The sentence turns on the day the chase does.

8 · Testing

  • lib/__tests__/offering-extract.test.ts: model JSON to draft rows, including the unknown-kind and empty cases
  • inferPolicy: one assertion per kind, pinning the §4 table
  • A test asserting the summary copy contains no waiver language while the Promise flag is off, so §7 cannot regress quietly
  • Fixtures from real messy input: "tuesday circle 8 guys 200/mo", "I run men's work", "coaching"

9 · Deliberately not built

  • Waitlist mechanics. Inference records the intent, P1 builds the behaviour
  • Session or calendar scheduling. Different object, different spec
  • Editing offerings after onboarding. AddOfferingForm already exists for that
  • Importing from Stripe, a website, or a calendar. Depends on integrations that do not exist

Next spec, not this one: the Promise wedge

Separate subsystem, separate brainstorm. Shape only, so the dependency in §7 has a visible path:

  • Object: a Promise is a document attached to an enrollment, with a state of unsent, sent, signed, or expired
  • Signing: click-to-sign with a typed name plus a timestamped PDF receipt emailed to both sides. Not DocuSign, not a legal product
  • The chase: unsigned promises surface in the Queue as decisions, and block attendance flags in the Brief
  • Why it is the wedge: Kajabi, Practice and Paperbell all do payments. None chase signatures, because none are shaped for live containers. A retreat legally cannot run without one. It is the single line of Speed's spec where no incumbent competes
  • Open question for that brainstorm: how much legal weight the receipt claims. There is a real difference between "we recorded that they agreed" and "this is an enforceable e-signature," and the second carries obligations we should not take on by accident
