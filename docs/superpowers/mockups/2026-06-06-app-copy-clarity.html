App copy is product copy

The playbook is aimed at the funnel. But a coach feels your brand most while using the app — every header, empty state, and button. Same nine laws. Here's how they make the app clearer, more directed, more useful.

1

Concrete, never abstract

Heath (Curse of Knowledge) · Sugarman (no multisyllabic words) · Schwartz (desire lives in concrete language)

Kill in-product jargon. A coach should never have to decode a brand word to use a button. "Embodiment" becomes "the breath at the third rib." Same in the UI.

Before
"Brand OS" · "Resonance pillars" · "Augmented Coach Cohort" · "command center"
After
"Your Voice" · "What you stand for" · "Coach Cohort" · "Home"
Where: nav, settings, Brand OS labels, onboarding. (You already fixed "command center" → "Home" — finish the set.)
2

Every empty state is a slippery slide

Sugarman (each line's only job: earn the next read) · Miller (name the next step)

An empty screen is your highest-intent moment — they showed up, there's nothing there yet. Never just state the absence. Name the one next move and make it feel inevitable.

Before
"No previous sessions with Ronnie Zepeda. The brief builds after the first session."
After
"First session with Ronnie. Save it, and his brief writes itself — what changed, what to send, what's next."
Where: every empty list (sessions, leads, quizzes, content, memory).
3

Empathy before authority

Miller (the Guide = empathy, then authority) · Halbert (write to ONE person)

Errors, gates, and onboarding should lead with "I get where you are," then direct. Never cold system-speak. Never a flat refusal.

Before
"I can't navigate the platform or open screens for you." · "Client not found"
After
"On it — opening your quizzes." · "That client isn't in your list yet. Add them first?"
Where: Dhara, error toasts, gates ("finish your Brand OS first"), 404s.
4

The header carries 80%

Bly (headline = 80%, the 4 U's: Useful, Ultra-specific, Unique, Urgent) · Heath (find the 4-word core)

Page and section titles are labels right now. Make them say what the coach gets, ultra-specifically. A title should sell the screen.

Before
"Funnels" · "Sessions" · "Content" · "Automations"
After (sub-headers / empty heroes)
"Quizzes that find your people" · "Every session, remembered" · "Show up in your voice" · "Follow-ups that send themselves"
Where: page sub-headers + empty-state heroes (keep nav labels short).
5

Speak to one person, in their state

Halbert (one reader) · Schwartz (stage of awareness) · Miller (SB7 three-layer problem)

Second person, singular, always. And meet them where they are: a first-run coach needs a hand; a power user wants it out of the way. You already nail this in Dhara ("You have 26 leads") — make it the house voice.

Before
"Users can create quizzes and manage leads." (feature voice)
After
"You've got 11 drafts waiting. Want to ship one?" (one coach, their moment)
Where: everywhere. Audit for "users / coaches can" → "you."
6

Honest is the strongest trigger

Sugarman (honesty + the "who this isn't for" disqualifier) · Cialdini (real scarcity, not fake)

No fake urgency, no dark patterns, ever — in-product or in marketing. When the app must say "this isn't ready" or "AI might be wrong," say it plainly. The honesty IS the brand (and Dhara's "candidate / unconfirmed" memory already lives this).

Before
"AI will extract topics, commitments, and patterns" (when it no longer does)
After
"Saved to this client's timeline." (true, plain — you already fixed this one)
Where: any copy that overpromises the AI; confidence labels; gates.
7

Two doors: a small step and a committed one

Miller (transitional CTA + direct CTA) · Brunson (value ladder)

Key screens should offer a low-commitment action next to the committed one, so no one bounces for lack of a small step.

Before
One button: "Generate quiz"
After
"Generate quiz" + a quiet "Draft from my brand instead" (you already do this — extend the pattern)
Where: quiz create, onboarding, the empty dashboard, paywall moments.

Where it pays off first

Three surfaces a coach hits constantly — fix the copy here and the whole app feels clearer.

1 · Empty states

Every "nothing here yet" becomes a slippery slide to the one next move (Law 2 + 4). Highest ROI, lowest risk.

2 · Jargon cull

"Brand OS / pillars / resonance / archetypes" → plain verbs in-product (Law 1). The clarity unlock you already started.

3 · Dhara + errors

Empathy-then-authority everywhere the app talks back (Law 3 + 5). Make the assistant the voice of the whole app.

1 · Copy clarity spec→ 2 · Empty-state + jargon pass→ 3 · Errors + onboarding→ 4 · Roll into the verify/jargon track
The strategic point: the funnel and the app should sound like the same man. A coach who reads honest, concrete, one-to-one copy on your landing page and then opens an app full of "Brand OS · pillars · command center" feels the seam. Closing that seam — same voice in the product as in the marketing — is the trust play, and trust is the whole resonance thesis. This is the same jargon-cull / verify-everywhere track from the Dhara roadmap, now with a real rubric behind it.
