Coach-App · Product Spec · v1

The Mirror

The first sticky surface. A coach brings what they are working through; the app names the kind of problem it is and the thinking-mistake they are about to make. Sticky on use #1, because day-one stickiness comes from the sharpness of one read, not the length of a history.

Elevate AI / Soma · Spec for build · 2026-06-16 · Engine: DART protocol (Recipe / Puzzle / Garden / Fire)

The bet

Day-one stickiness = the called-out moment. One sharp read of the thing they are stuck on right now. "You are treating a Garden like a Recipe. That is why it will not move." No history needed. The misclassification is visible in a single input.

Week-3 stickiness = the moat. The same engine quietly accrues every read. By week three it reflects their pattern back: "you always bring a checklist to a Garden." A rival can copy the feature, never the history. Hook now, moat later.

1The surface & interaction

A front-door card on the coach's home, never the word "Cynefin." One open prompt, one text box, no form.

1
Coach types the stuck thing in their own words. "I've been redoing my funnel for three weeks and nothing converts." One to three sentences.
2
Guardrail gate. If the read is thin or could go two ways, the Mirror asks one clarifying question first. It never guesses.
3
The Mirror returns three things: the kind of problem (plain metaphor), the mistake they are making (the called-out line), and the one move that fits the right protocol.
4
Coach taps "that's me" or "not quite." That tap is the investment: it feeds the compounding mirror and trains precision.

Return trigger: a daily/weekly nudge ("what are you working through?") plus the accumulating mirror they can scroll. The hook line lives in every single use; the pattern line arrives weekly.

2The three reads over time

Same engine. Stickiness deepens as data accrues, but it is never flat, because read one already lands.

Use #1 · minute one
Single-shot mirror. Names the kind of problem + the mistake on the thing they brought today. The "how did it know that about me" jolt. Zero history required.
End of week 1
Within-week mirror. "This week you brought 4 fires and 1 garden. You tried to checklist the garden twice." Real with seven days of taps.
Week 3 and beyond
Cross-week identity pattern. "You always reach for a checklist when you are scared of an emergent problem." The moat. No competitor has this on them.

3The engine prompt

The classify-and-name-the-mistake system prompt. Claude (latest), structured JSON out. Returning users also pass their recent classification history to sharpen the read; not required day one.

# SYSTEM You are The Mirror, a thinking partner inside a coach's app. A coach brings you something they are working through. Your job is NOT to solve it. It is to name what KIND of problem it is and the thinking-mistake they are likely making, so they apply the right protocol. Four kinds (use the plain names, never Clear/Complicated/Complex/Chaotic): • Recipe cause+effect obvious, linear, known steps exist. → follow the recipe, ship, do not be clever. • Puzzle cause+effect hidden but knowable with expertise. → slow down, get the exact specialist, diagnose. • Garden cause+effect emergent, clear only in hindsight, shifts with human behavior. → small experiments, one variable, measure, adjust. No checklist works. • Fire the link is broken, things move fast. → stabilize first, contain, diagnose after. The mistake to catch (the sharp part): people apply the wrong protocol to the right problem. Watch how they describe it: • optimizing / redoing / perfecting / "I keep tweaking" on a Garden → they are checklisting a Garden. (most common) • researching / waiting for certainty on a Fire → studying a fire instead of putting it out. • winging it / reinventing / over-thinking on a Recipe → experimenting on something with a known recipe. • guessing / hiring a generalist on a Puzzle → they need the exact specialist, not more effort. Rules: • Be sharp, not dramatic. One clean read beats three hedges. • If input is thin or could be two kinds, DO NOT guess. Ask one question. • Only name a mistake if you SEE the mismatch signal. No signal → no "gotcha." A wrong call destroys trust faster than a missed one. • If this reads as real personal crisis or distress, drop the framework. Respond as a human, point to stabilize and to a person, not a protocol. • Grounded coach voice. No corporate tone. No em-dashes. # RETURN (JSON) { confidence, needs_clarification, clarifying_question, system_type, type_reason, the_mirror_line, the_mistake, the_move, is_crisis }

5The guardrail · against confident-wrong

Day-one stickiness is a knife. A sharp read that's wrong kills trust on use #1 and they never return. Five protections:

  1. Confidence gate. Below 0.7, the Mirror asks one clarifying question instead of labelling. Never a verdict on thin input.
  2. Hypothesis, not verdict. The read is framed as something they confirm. The "that's me / not quite" tap is built into the UI, not an afterthought.
  3. No invented mistakes. the_mistake is null unless the mismatch signal is actually present. A forced gotcha that misses is worse than none.
  4. Crisis override. If it reads as real distress, not a business problem, drop the framework entirely. Respond human, route to stabilize and to a person. Never gamify a crisis. (Embodiment ethic, non-negotiable.)
  5. Calibration loop. "Not quite" taps are logged and reviewed. Low-precision patterns tighten the prompt. The guardrail trains the engine over time.

6Build order

Phase 1 · ship first

Instant mirror

Front-door card + text box → Claude endpoint → the read + taps. Log every read to Supabase. This alone is day-one sticky. Ship it as a standalone, test stickiness in a week.

Phase 2

Within-week mirror

Weekly digest card + email: counts by type + the within-week catch ("checklisted the garden twice"). Runs off the logged reads. No new model work.

Phase 3

The moat

Cross-week identity patterns. Feed history back into the prompt so each read is personalized. This is the switching cost; it only works once the log is deep.

Lean stack: Next.js route in coach-app → Claude (latest) with the prompt above → JSON → render. One Supabase table mirror_reads (coach_id, input, system_type, the_mistake, confidence, tap, ts). No dependency on the dormant Deposit Engine. The same mirror_reads table is what Phases 2–3 read.

The one assumption + open questions

Assumption I made to keep this moving: The Mirror is a standalone front-door surface with a fresh lightweight Claude endpoint — not woven into the Brand OS onboarding flow, not waiting on the Deposit Engine. Fastest path to a real day-one stickiness test. If you'd rather it live inside Brand OS or the weekly check-in ritual, the spec shifts.

  • Trigger cadence: daily nudge, or only when they choose to open it? (Daily nudge = more shots at the hook, more risk of nag.)
  • Does it carry the Soma name/2-layer UX, or ship plain first and rebrand later?
  • Free (top-of-funnel, like Brand OS) or gated behind a tier? Day-one stickiness argues free.
